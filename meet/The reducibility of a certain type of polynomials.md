>[!question]
>For $m>n\geq1$, determine the reducibility of $x^{n}\pm x^{m}\pm 1$.

>[!definition] Definition
>The **reciprocal polynomial** $f^{*}(x)$ of a polynomial $f(x)$ is defined as $f^{*}(x)=x^{\operatorname{deg}f}f(x^{-1})$.
>
>Intuitively, it reverses the order of the coefficients of $f(x)$.


>[!theorem] Theorem 1
>Let $f(x)\in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ and $f(0)\neq 0$, $f(x)$ and $f^{*}(x)$ has no common roots (i.e. $\operatorname{gcd}(f,f^{*})=1$). If $f(x)$ is reducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, then there exists $p(x)\in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ other than $\pm f(x),\pm f^{*}(x)$ such that $\operatorname{deg}p(x)=\operatorname{deg}f(x)$, and $f(x)f^{*}(x)=p(x)p^{*}(x)$.
>
>Furthermore, if $f(x)$ is monic and $f(0)=\pm 1$, then we can require $p(x)$ to be monic.


>[!theorem] Theorem 2
>$f(x)=x^{n}\pm x^{m}\pm 1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ if and only if it has a common root with $f^{*}(x)$.
