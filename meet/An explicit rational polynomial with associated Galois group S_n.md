>[!theorem] Theorem 0
>Take $n\geq 2$, denote $K$ the splitting field of $f(x)=x^{n}-x-1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, then $\operatorname{Gal}(K|\mathbb{Q})\cong S_{n}$.

To begin with, we clarify some notions. $\mathcal{O}_{K}$ is the integral closure of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $K$. $\Delta_K$ is the discriminant of $\mathcal{O}_{K}$ and $\operatorname{disc}(f)$ is the discriminant of $f(x)$.
___

>[!proposition] Proposition 1
>$$
>\operatorname{disc}(f)=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}(n^{n}+(-n+1)^{n-1})
>$$

>[!lemma] Lemma 1
>Let $p$ be a prime number and $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime ideal in $K$ satisfying $\mathfrak{p} \mid p$. If $f(x) \equiv (x-c)^2 \overline{h}(x) \pmod{p}$ for some $c \in \mathbb{Z}$ and a separable polynomial $\overline{h}(x) \pmod{p}$ such that $\overline{h}(c) \not\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$, then the inertia group of $\mathfrak{p}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is either trivial or a group generated by a transposition.

**Proof:**
Since $f(x) \equiv (x-c)^2 \overline{h}(x) \pmod{p}$, Hensel's lemma shows that $f(x) = g(x) h(x)$ in the rational $p$-adic number field $\mathbb{Q}_p$ such that $g(x) \equiv (x-c)^2 \pmod{p}$ and $h(x) \equiv \overline{h}(x) \pmod{p}$. Let $K_{\mathfrak{p}}$ be the $\mathfrak{p}$-completion of $K$. $K_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is obtained from $\mathbb{Q}_p$ by adjoining the roots of $f(x)$. The roots of $h(x)$ generate an unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Hence, if $K_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is ramified over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, then the inertia group of $\mathfrak{p}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is a group generated by the transposition of the roots of $g(x)$. This completes the proof.
___

>[!lemma] Lemma 2
>Let $p$ be a prime and $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime ideal in $\mathcal{O}_{K}$ with $p \in \mathfrak{p}$, then the inertia group of $\mathfrak{p}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is either trivial or a group generated by a transposition.

**Proof:**
If $\mathfrak{p}$ is ramified, then $p\mid \operatorname{disc}(f)$, thus by proposition 1, $p \not\mid n,n-1$. Therefore, $f'(x)\equiv nx^{n-1}-1 \pmod{p})$ has no multiple root, and hence every irreducible factor of $f(x) \pmod{p}$ has multiplicity at most two.

Suppose $\alpha$ is a multiple root of $f(x)$ in the splitting field of $f(x)$ is $\mathbb{F}_{p}$. Then $n\alpha^{n-1}-1=0$ and $\alpha^{n}-\alpha-1=0$, which implies $\alpha=-n(n-1)^{-1}$, thus $f(x)$ has a unique multiple root. Applying lemma 1 completes the proof.
