> [!problem]
> For $f: S^1 \to S^1$, $f(z)=z^2$, with notation $\sigma, s, \tau$ as defined, prove that $\sigma^2 - \sigma + s - \tau$ is a 1-boundary in $C_1(S^1)$.

**Proof**:
Since $V$ is contractible, the two paths $\sigma^2$ and $\tau$ (both from $f(e^{i(\alpha_0-\delta)})$ to $f(e^{i(\alpha_0+\delta)})$ are homotopic in $V$. Therefore, $\sigma^2 - \tau = \partial c_1$ for some $c_1 \in C_2(S^1)$.

Similarly, $\sigma$ and $s$ are homotopic in $S^1$ relative to endpoints (after adjusting parameterization), so $\sigma - s = \partial c_2$ for some $c_2 \in C_2(S^1)$.
> 
> Let $c = c_1 - c_2$. Then:
> 
> $\partial c = \partial c_1 - \partial c_2 = (\sigma^2 - \tau) - (\sigma - s) = \sigma^2 - \sigma + s - \tau$
> 
> Hence $\sigma^2 - \sigma + s - \tau$ is a 1-boundary. $\square$
