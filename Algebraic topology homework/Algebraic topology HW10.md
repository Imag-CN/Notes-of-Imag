> [!problem] [HAT] 2.1.30
>In each of the following commutative diagrams, assume that all maps but one are isomorphisms. Show that the remaining map must be an isomorphism as well.
>
>```tikz
>\usepackage{tikz-cd}
>
>\begin{document}
\begin{tikzcd}
A \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & B \\
C \arrow[ur] 
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}
A \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & B \arrow[d] \\
C \arrow[r] & D
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}
A \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & B \\
C \arrow[r] & D \arrow[u]
\end{tikzcd}
>\end{document}
>```

**Proof:**
Since the given maps are isomorphisms, they are invertible. By the commutativity of the diagram, the remaining map can be expressed as a composition of these isomorphisms (or their inverses). Therefore, it is itself an isomorphism.

> [!problem] [HAT] 2.1.18
> (a) Compute the homology groups $H_{n}(X, A)$ when $X$ is $S^{2}$ or $S^{1} \times S^{1}$ and $A$ is a finite set of points in $X$.
>
> (b) Compute the groups $H_{n}(X, A)$ and $H_{n}(X, B)$ for $X$ a closed orientable surface of genus two with $A$ and $B$ the circles shown. [What are $X / A$ and $X / B$?]

**Proof:**
**(a)** For $X = S^2$, $A = m$ points, the long exact sequence for $(X, A)$ gives:
- $H_2(S^2, A) \cong H_2(S^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
- $H_1(S^2, A) \cong \ker (H_0(A) \to H_0(S^2)) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{m-1}$ (since $H_0(A) \cong \mathbb{Z}^m$, and the map is summation to $\mathbb{Z}$).
- $H_0(S^2, A) = 0$ because $S^2$ is path-connected.
Thus $H_n(S^2, A) = \mathbb{Z}$ for $n=2$, $\mathbb{Z}^{m-1}$ for $n=1$, and $0$ otherwise.

For $X = S^1 \times S^1$, $A = m$ points, the long exact sequence for $(X, A)$ gives:
- $H_2(S^1 \times S^1, A) \cong H_2(S^1 \times S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
- $H_0(S^1 \times S^1, A) = 0$.
- $H_1(S^1 \times S^1, A) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{m+1}$

**(b)** Notice that $(X,A)$ and $(X,B)$ are good pairs, hence $H_n(X,A) \cong H_n(X/A)$ and $H_n(X,B) \cong H_n(X/B)$.  
But $X/A \cong T^2 \vee T^2 \cong T^2 \sqcup T^2 / \{x,y\}$, hence $H_n(X,A) = H_n(T^2,\{x\}) \oplus H_n(T^2,\{y\})$.  
By previous part,  
$$
H_n(T^2,\{x\}) = 
\begin{cases}
\mathbb{Z} & \text{when } n = 2 \\
\mathbb{Z}^2 & \text{when } n = 1 \\
0 & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$  
Thus  
$$
H_n(X,A) = 
\begin{cases}
\mathbb{Z}^2 & \text{when } n = 2 \\
\mathbb{Z}^4 & \text{when } n = 1 \\
0 & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Similarly, $X/B \cong T^2 / \{x,y\}$, hence $H_n(X,B) = H_n(T^2,\{x\}) \oplus H_n(T^2,\{x,y\})$.

Therefore,  
$$
H_n(X,B) = 
\begin{cases}
\mathbb{Z} & \text{when } n = 2 \\
\mathbb{Z}^3 & \text{when } n = 1 \\
0 & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

> [!problem] [HAT] 2.1.18
> Show that for the subspace $\mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{R}$, the relative homology group $H_1(\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{Q})$ is free abelian and find a basis.

**Proof:**
Use the long exact sequence for $(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Q})$:

$$
\cdots \to H_1(\mathbb{R}) \to H_1(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Q}) \to H_0(\mathbb{Q}) \xrightarrow{\alpha} H_0(\mathbb{R}) \to \cdots
$$

Since $\mathbb{R}$ is contractible, $H_1(\mathbb{R})=0$ and $H_0(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{Z}$. $H_0(\mathbb{Q}) \cong \bigoplus_{q\in\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Z}$, generated by points $[q]$.

The map $\alpha$ is the summation $\alpha((n_q)) = \sum_q n_q$. Hence $\ker\alpha \cong H_1(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Q})$.

Fix $q_0\in\mathbb{Q}$ (e.g. $q_0=0$). Then $\{e_q := [q]-[q_0] \mid q\neq q_0\}$ is a basis for $\ker\alpha$.

Thus $H_1(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Q})$ is free abelian with basis $\{e_q\}_{q\neq q_0}$.
