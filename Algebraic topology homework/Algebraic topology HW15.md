>[!problem]
> Prove that for all integers $n$ and all CW complexes $X$, we have $H^n(X, X^n) = 0$.

**Proof:**
Consider the triple $(X,X^{n+1},X^n)$. The long exact sequence gives:
$$
H^n(X^{n+1},X^n) \to H^n(X,X^n) \to H^n(X,X^{n+1})
$$
Since $X^{n+1}/X^n$ is a wedge of $(n+1)$-spheres, its $n$-th cohomology vanishes, so $H^n(X^{n+1},X^n)=0$. The quotient $X/X^{n+1}$ has no cells below dimension $n+2$, so $H^n(X,X^{n+1}) \cong H^n(X/X^{n+1}) = 0$. Exactness then forces $H^n(X,X^n)=0$.

>[!problem] [HAT] 3.1.8
>Directly from the definitions, compute the simplicial cohomology groups of $S^1 \times S^1$ with $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}_2$ coefficients, using the $\Delta$-complex structure given in §2.1.
>
>Do the same for $\mathbb{R}P^2$ and the Klein bottle.

**Proof:**
**1. Torus $S^1\times S^1$**
$\Delta$-complex: one 0-simplex $v$, three 1-simplices $a,b,c$, two 2-simplices $U,L$ with $\partial_2U=\partial_2L=a+b-c$.

$C^0=G$, $C^1=G^3$, $C^2=G^2$.

- $G=\mathbb{Z}$: $\delta^0=0$, $H^0\cong\mathbb{Z}$. $\delta^1\psi(U)=\delta^1\psi(L)=p+q-r$, so $\ker\delta^1\cong\mathbb{Z}^2$, $H^1\cong\mathbb{Z}^2$. $\operatorname{im}\delta^1\cong\mathbb{Z}$, $H^2\cong\mathbb{Z}^2/\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}$.
- $G=\mathbb{Z}_2$: Same, $H^0\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$, $H^1\cong\mathbb{Z}_2^2$, $H^2\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$.

**2. $\mathbb{R}P^2$**
$\Delta$-complex: one 0-simplex $v$, one 1-simplex $a$, one 2-simplex $U$ with $\partial_2U=2a$.

$C^0=C^1=C^2=G$.

- $G=\mathbb{Z}$: $\delta^0=0$, $H^0\cong\mathbb{Z}$. $\delta^1(x)=2x$, so $\ker\delta^1=0$, $H^1=0$. $\operatorname{im}\delta^1=2\mathbb{Z}$, $H^2\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$.
- $G=\mathbb{Z}_2$: $H^0\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}$ $\delta^1(x)=2x=0$, so $\ker\delta^1\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$, $H^1\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$. $\operatorname{im}\delta^1=0$, $H^2\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$.

**3. Klein bottle**
$\Delta$-complex: one 0-simplex $v$, three 1-simplices $a,b,c$, two 2-simplices $U,L$ with $\partial_2U=a+b-c$, $\partial_2L=a-b+c$.

$C^0=G$, $C^1=G^3$, $C^2=G^2$.

- $G=\mathbb{Z}$: $\delta^0=0$, $H^0\cong\mathbb{Z}$. $\delta^1\psi(U)=p+q-r$, $\delta^1\psi(L)=p-q+r$. Solving $p+q-r=0$, $p-q+r=0$ gives $p=0$, $q=r$, so $\ker\delta^1\cong\mathbb{Z}$, $H^1\cong\mathbb{Z}$. $\operatorname{im}\delta^1$ generated by $(1,1),(1,-1)$, index 2 sublattice, so $H^2\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$.
- $G=\mathbb{Z}_2$: $H^0\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}$. $\delta^1\psi(U)=\delta^1\psi(L)=p+q+r$. $\ker\delta^1\cong\mathbb{Z}_2^2$, $H^1\cong\mathbb{Z}_2^2$. $\operatorname{im}\delta^1\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$, $H^2\cong\mathbb{Z}_2^2/\mathbb{Z}_2\cong\mathbb{Z}_2$.

>[!problem] [HAT] 3.1.8
>Many basic homology arguments work just as well for cohomology even though maps go in the opposite direction. Verify this in the following cases:
>
>Compute $H^i(S^n; G)$ by induction on $n$ in two ways:
>- using the long exact sequence of a pair, and
>- using the Mayer–Vietoris sequence.
>
>Show that if $A$ is a closed subspace of $X$ that is a deformation retract of some neighborhood, then the quotient map $X \to X/A$ induces isomorphisms
>$$
>H^n(X, A; G) \cong \widetilde{H}^n(X/A; G)
>$$
>for all $n$.
>
>Show that if $A$ is a retract of $X$, then
>$$
>H^n(X; G) \cong H^n(A; G) \oplus H^n(X, A; G).
>$$

**Proof:**
**(i)** Two proofs that $H^i(S^n;G) \cong H^{i-1}(S^{n-1};G)$.

*Proof 1 (long exact sequence of a pair)*: Consider $(D^n,S^{n-1})$. Since $D^n$ is contractible, $H^i(D^n;G)=0$ for $i>0$. The long exact sequence gives $H^i(S^{n-1};G) \cong H^{i+1}(D^n,S^{n-1};G)$. Since $(D^n,S^{n-1})$ is a good pair, $H^{i+1}(D^n,S^{n-1};G) \cong H^{i+1}(S^n;G)$. Hence $H^{i+1}(S^n;G) \cong H^i(S^{n-1};G)$.

*Proof 2 (Mayer-Vietoris)*: Write $S^n = A \cup B$ where $A,B$ are contractible neighborhoods of the northern and southern hemispheres, so $A \cap B \simeq S^{n-1}$. The Mayer-Vietoris sequence gives $H^{i-1}(S^{n-1};G) \cong H^i(S^n;G)$.

**(ii)** Let $q:(X,A) \to (X/A,A/A)$ be the quotient map. Since $A$ is a deformation retract of a neighborhood, $q$ induces an isomorphism $H_n(X,A) \cong H_n(X/A,A/A)$ for all $n$. Apply the Universal Coefficient Theorem to both pairs; the naturality of UCT gives a commutative diagram with exact rows. The left and right vertical maps are isomorphisms, so by the Five Lemma, $q^*: H^n(X/A,A/A;G) \to H^n(X,A;G)$ is an isomorphism. Since $A/A$ is a point, $H^n(X/A,A/A;G) \cong \tilde{H}^n(X/A;G)$.

**(iii)** Let $r:X \to A$ be a retraction, $i:A \hookrightarrow X$. Then $r \circ i = \operatorname{id}_A$, so $i_*$ is injective and the long exact sequence of $(X,A)$ splits: $H_n(X) \cong H_n(A) \oplus H_n(X,A)$. Applying the Universal Coefficient Theorem to $X$, $A$, and $(X,A)$, the splitting passes through UCT and the Five Lemma gives $H^n(X;G) \cong H^n(A;G) \oplus H^n(X,A;G)$.

>[!problem] [HAT] 3.1.9
>Show that if $f: S^n \to S^n$ has degree $d$, then $f^*: H^n(S^n; G) \to H^n(S^n; G)$ is multiplication by $d$.

**Proof:**
By the Universal Coefficient Theorem, the map $h: \tilde{H}^n(S^n;G) \to \operatorname{Hom}(\tilde{H}_n(S^n),G)$ is an isomorphism since $\tilde{H}_{n-1}(S^n)=0$. Naturality gives a commutative diagram:

$$
\begin{CD}
\tilde{H}^n(S^n;G) @>h>> \operatorname{Hom}(\tilde{H}_n(S^n),G) \\
@V{f^*}VV @V{(f_*)^*}VV \\
\tilde{H}^n(S^n;G) @>h>> \operatorname{Hom}(\tilde{H}_n(S^n),G)
\end{CD}
$$

Since $f$ has degree $d$, $f_*: \tilde{H}_n(S^n) \to \tilde{H}_n(S^n)$ is multiplication by $d$. Hence $(f_*)^*$ is multiplication by $d$ on $\operatorname{Hom}(\tilde{H}_n(S^n),G)$. Commutativity then gives $f^* = h^{-1} \circ (f_*)^* \circ h$, so $f^*$ is multiplication by $d$.
