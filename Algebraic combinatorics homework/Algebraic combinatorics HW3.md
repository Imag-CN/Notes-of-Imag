> [!problem] 9.1
> Let $\lambda$ be a partition and $\lambda'$ its conjugate. Prove that
> $$
> s_\lambda(\mathbf{x})=\det(M)
> $$
> where $M$ is a $\lambda_1\times\lambda_1$ matrix and $M_{i,j}=e_{\lambda'_j-j+i}(\mathbf{x})$.

**Proof:**
Let $l=\lambda_1$, and set
$$
u_i=(1-i,1),\qquad v_i=(\lambda'_i-i+1,\infty)
$$
for $1\le i\le l$. Consider northeast lattice paths from the $u_i$'s to the $v_j$'s.

Use the $e$-labeling of the east steps: if the east steps of a path, from left to right, occur at heights
$$
a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_r,
$$
give the path weight $x_{a_1}\cdots x_{a_r}$. Thus the weight-generating function of paths having $r$ east steps is $e_r(\mathbf{x})$.

A path from $u_i$ to $v_j$ has exactly
$$
(\lambda'_j-j+1)-(1-i)=\lambda'_j-j+i
$$
east steps. Hence its weight-generating function is
$$
e_{\lambda'_j-j+i}(\mathbf{x}).
$$
Therefore, by the LGV lemma,
$$
\det\left(e_{\lambda'_j-j+i}(\mathbf{x})\right)_{1\le i,j\le l}
$$
is the weight-generating function of the nonintersecting path families connecting $u_i$ to $v_i$.

It remains to identify these families with semistandard Young tableaux of shape $\lambda$. Given a nonintersecting family $(P_1,\ldots,P_l)$, record the labels of the east steps of $P_j$ in the $j$-th column of $\lambda$, from top to bottom. Since the $e$-labels along each path are strictly increasing, the entries in each column are strictly increasing. Since the paths are nonintersecting, the entries in each row are weakly increasing. Thus we obtain a semistandard Young tableau of shape $\lambda$.

Conversely, from a semistandard Young tableau of shape $\lambda$, the entries in its $j$-th column uniquely determine the east steps of $P_j$. Column-strictness gives a valid path, and row weak increase implies that the resulting paths are nonintersecting. Hence this is a weight-preserving bijection.

Therefore the generating function of the nonintersecting families is
$$
\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SSYT}(\lambda)}\mathbf{x}^T=s_\lambda(\mathbf{x}).
$$
Consequently,
$$
s_\lambda(\mathbf{x})
=
\det\left(e_{\lambda'_j-j+i}(\mathbf{x})\right)_{1\le i,j\le\lambda_1}
=
\det(M).
$$
>[!remark] Remark
>Applying $\omega$ to the identity gives $\omega(s_{\lambda})=s_{\lambda'}$.

> [!problem] 9.2
> Prove in two ways that
> $$
> s_{(k,1^{n-k})}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-k}(-1)^i h_{k+i}(\mathbf{x})e_{n-k-i}(\mathbf{x});
> $$
> - using a sign reversing involution
> - by expanding the Jacobi-Trudi determinant along the first column.

**Proof:**
Put $r=n-k$.

**First proof:**
A monomial in $h_{k+i}e_{r-i}$ can be represented by a pair
$$
(a_1\le\cdots\le a_{k+i};\,b_1<\cdots<b_{r-i}),
$$
with weight $x_{a_1}\cdots x_{a_{k+i}}x_{b_1}\cdots x_{b_{r-i}}$ and sign $(-1)^i$.

We define a sign-reversing involution as follows. Compare $a_1$ with the entries $b_j$. If $a_1<b_1$ (or if there is no $b_1$), move $a_1$ from the weakly increasing sequence to the strictly increasing sequence. Otherwise, let $b_j$ be the largest $b_j\le a_1$ and move $b_j$ from the strictly increasing sequence to the weakly increasing sequence.

The move changes $i$ by $1$, preserves the weight, and reversing the same move recovers the original pair. Hence all terms cancel except when $i=0$ and
$$
a_1\le\cdots\le a_k,\qquad
a_1<b_1<\cdots<b_r.
$$
These are precisely the semistandard tableaux of hook shape $(k,1^r)$, with the $a_j$ in the first row and the $b_j$ below $a_1$ in the first column. Therefore
$$
\sum_{i=0}^r(-1)^ih_{k+i}e_{r-i}=s_{(k,1^r)}.
$$
**Second proof:**
Use the Jacobi-Trudi determinant
$$
s_\lambda=\det\left(h_{\lambda_j-j+i}\right).
$$
For $\lambda=(k,1^r)$, its first column is
$$
(h_k,h_{k+1},\ldots,h_{k+r})^T.
$$
Expanding along this column, the cofactor corresponding to $h_{k+i}$ has sign $(-1)^i$, and its minor is the Jacobi-Trudi determinant for $(1^{r-i})$, hence equals $e_{r-i}$. Therefore
$$
s_{(k,1^r)}
=\sum_{i=0}^r(-1)^ih_{k+i}e_{r-i}.
$$
Since $r=n-k$, this is
$$
s_{(k,1^{n-k})}
=\sum_{i=0}^{n-k}(-1)^ih_{k+i}e_{n-k-i}.
$$

**Background**

Let $P$ be a finite, finite-depth poset that is a **rooted tree** (growing downwards from a unique root $\hat{0}$), where every element $x\in P$ has a unique covered element except the root.

We define the **ideal** generated by $x$ as the set of all descendants of $x$:
$$
L(x)=\{y\in P:y\ge x\}.
$$
The size of this ideal, $h(x)=|L(x)|$, is the **hook length** of the element $x$ in the tree.

A **linear extension** of $P$ is a bijection $f:P\to\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$, where $n=|P|$, such that if $x<y$ in $P$, then $f(x)<f(y)$. Let $\mathcal{L}(P)$ denote the set of all linear extensions of $P$. Knuth's hook length formula for trees states that:
$$
|\mathcal{L}(P)|=\frac{n!}{\prod_{x\in P}h(x)}.
$$

Let $P$ be a rooted tree poset with $n$ elements. Consider the following probabilistic process, called the **Tree Hook Walk**:

 1. **Initialization:** Choose an element $x_0\in P$ uniformly at random, such that $\mathbb{P}(x_0=x)=\frac{1}{n}$.

2. **Transition:** If $x_k$ is a maximal element of $P$, terminate the walk and set the terminal element $z=x_k$. If $x_k$ is not maximal, choose the next element $x_{k+1}$ from the strict upper ideal $L(x_k)\setminus\{x_k\}$ uniformly at random:
$$
\mathbb{P}(x_{k+1}=y\mid x_k)=\frac{1}{h(x_k)-1}\quad\text{for }y>x_k.
$$
3. **Iteration:** Repeat Step 2 until the walk terminates at a maximal element $z$.

Now you need to prove Knuth's hook length formula:

> [!problem] 10.1
> Prove that, for any maximal element $z\in P$, the probability $P(z)$ that the Tree Hook Walk terminates at $z$ is given by:
> $$
> P(z)=\frac{1}{n}\prod_{x<z}\left(1+\frac{1}{h(x)-1}\right).
> $$

**Proof:**
Fix a maximal element $z$. For $x\le z$, let $q(x)$ be the probability that the walk ever visits $x$.

The walk can visit $x$ either by starting at $x$, with probability $1/n$, or by jumping to $x$ from some $y<x$. Hence
$$
q(x)=\frac1n+\sum_{y<x}\frac{q(y)}{h(y)-1}.
$$
Since $P$ is a rooted tree, the elements below $x$ form a chain. Write them as $x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_r=x$. Then
$$
q(x_i)=\frac1n+\sum_{j<i}\frac{q(x_j)}{h(x_j)-1}.
$$
Subtracting the equations for $i$ and $i-1$ gives
$$
q(x_i)=q(x_{i-1})\left(1+\frac1{h(x_{i-1})-1}\right).
$$
Since $q(x_0)=1/n$, iterating gives
$$
q(x)=\frac1n\prod_{y<x}\left(1+\frac1{h(y)-1}\right).
$$
Since $z$ is maximal, the walk terminates at $z$ exactly when it visits $z$. Therefore
$$
P(z)=\frac1n\prod_{x<z}\left(1+\frac1{h(x)-1}\right).
$$

> [!problem] 10.2
> Let $P\setminus\{z\}$ denote the poset obtained by deleting a maximal element $z$. Prove by induction on $n$ that:
> $$\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in\max(P)}\prod_{x\in P\setminus\{z\}}\frac{1}{h_{P\setminus\{z\}}(x)}=\prod_{x\in P}\frac{1}{h_P(x)}.$$
>
> Conclude that this identity implies Knuth's hook length formula for trees: $|\mathcal{L}(P)|=n!/\prod_{x\in P}h(x)$.

**Proof:**
Fix a maximal element $z$. Deleting $z$ decreases the hook length by $1$ exactly for $x<z$. Hence
$$
h_{P\setminus\{z\}}(x)=
\begin{cases}
h_P(x)-1,&x<z,\\
h_P(x),&x\nless z,\ x\ne z.
\end{cases}
$$
Since $h_P(z)=1$,
$$
\prod_{x\in P\setminus\{z\}}\frac1{h_{P\setminus\{z\}}(x)}
=
\left(\prod_{x\in P}\frac1{h_P(x)}\right)
\prod_{x<z}\frac{h_P(x)}{h_P(x)-1}.
$$
By 9.1,
$$
\frac1n\prod_{x<z}\frac{h_P(x)}{h_P(x)-1}=P(z).
$$
Thus
$$
\frac1n\prod_{x\in P\setminus\{z\}}\frac1{h_{P\setminus\{z\}}(x)}
=
P(z)\prod_{x\in P}\frac1{h_P(x)}.
$$
Summing over all maximal $z$ and using $\sum_zP(z)=1$ gives
$$
\frac1n\sum_{z\in\max(P)}
\prod_{x\in P\setminus\{z\}}\frac1{h_{P\setminus\{z\}}(x)}
=
\prod_{x\in P}\frac1{h_P(x)}.
$$

Finally, in every linear extension, the element labeled $n$ is maximal. Conversely, choosing a maximal $z$ to receive the label $n$ and a linear extension of $P\setminus\{z\}$ uniquely determines a linear extension of $P$. Therefore
$$
|\mathcal L(P)|=\sum_{z\in\max(P)}|\mathcal L(P\setminus\{z\})|.
$$
By induction,
$$
|\mathcal L(P\setminus\{z\})|
=(n-1)!\prod_{x\in P\setminus\{z\}}\frac1{h_{P\setminus\{z\}}(x)}.
$$
Hence, using the identity above,
$$
|\mathcal L(P)|
=(n-1)!\sum_{z\in\max(P)}
\prod_{x\in P\setminus\{z\}}\frac1{h_{P\setminus\{z\}}(x)}
=n!\prod_{x\in P}\frac1{h_P(x)}.
$$

Let $\beta(\ell,\lambda)$ be the number of walks of length $\ell$ on Young's lattice starting at $\varnothing$ and ending at $\lambda$ using up and down steps.

>[!problem] 11.1
>Prove that $\beta(\ell,\lambda)=0$ if $\ell-|\lambda|$ is odd.

**Proof:**
Take any walk of length $\ell$ on Young's lattice starting at $\varnothing$ and ending at $\lambda$. Let $u$ and $d$ be the numbers of up and down steps, respectively. Then $\ell=u+d$ and $|\lambda|=u-d$. Hence
$$
\ell-|\lambda|=2d
$$
is even. Therefore, if $\ell-|\lambda|$ is odd, no such walk exists, so $\beta(\ell,\lambda)=0$.
___

>[!problem] 11.2
>Prove that $\beta(|\lambda|,\lambda)=f^\lambda$ where $f^\lambda$ is the number of standard Young tableaux of shape $\lambda$.

**Proof:**
A walk of length $|\lambda|$ from $\varnothing$ to $\lambda$ must have $d=0$, since $|\lambda|=u-d$ and $|\lambda|=u+d$. Thus it consists entirely of up steps.

Such a walk is a saturated chain
$$
\varnothing=\lambda^{(0)}\lessdot\lambda^{(1)}\lessdot\cdots\lessdot\lambda^{(|\lambda|)}=\lambda.
$$
There is a bijection between these chains and standard Young tableaux of shape $\lambda$: label the cell added at the $k$-th step by $k$. Therefore
$$
\beta(|\lambda|,\lambda)=f^\lambda.
$$

>[!problem] 11.3
>Prove that $\beta(\ell,\lambda)=\langle\lambda|(U+D)^\ell|\varnothing\rangle$.

**Proof:**
By definition,
$$
U|\mu\rangle=\sum_{\nu\gtrdot\mu}|\nu\rangle,
\qquad
D|\mu\rangle=\sum_{\nu\lessdot\mu}|\nu\rangle.
$$
Hence $(U+D)|\mu\rangle$ is the sum of all partitions reachable from $\mu$ by one up or down step. Therefore the coefficient of $|\lambda\rangle$ in $(U+D)^\ell|\varnothing\rangle$ is exactly the number of walks of length $\ell$ from $\varnothing$ to $\lambda$. Thus
$$
\beta(\ell,\lambda)=\langle\lambda|(U+D)^\ell|\varnothing\rangle.
$$
