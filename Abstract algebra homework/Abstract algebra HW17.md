>[!problem] Problem 1
>Relate the concept of greatest common divisor and least common multiple with sum and intersection of ideals of the ring $\mathbb{Z}$.

**Proof:**
Let $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}^+$, and let ideals be of the form $(n),n\in \mathbb{\mathbb{N}}$, then
$$
(a)+(b)= (a, b) = (\gcd(a, b)),
$$

$$
(a) \cap (b) = (\operatorname{lcm}(a, b)).
$$

>[!problem] Problem 2
>Let $R=\mathbb{Z}[x]$.
>
>(i) Prove that $R$ is an integral domain.
>
>(ii) Describe the fraction field of $R$.
>
>(iii) Find an ideal of $R$ that cannot be generated by one element.
>
>(iv) Describe all maximal ideals of $R$.

**Proof:**
**(i)** The polynomial ring over an integral domain is an integral domain.
**(ii)** $\mathbb{Q}(x)$.
**(iii)** $(2,x)$.
**(iv)** Maximal ideals of $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ are of the form $(p,f(x))$ where $p$ is a prime number and $f(x)$ is a polynomial in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ that is irreducible modulo $p$. Equivalently, they are ideals that are maximal with respect to containing $(p)$ for some prime $p$.

>[!problem] Problem 3
>The Krull dimension of a ring $A$ is defined to be the maximal $n$ such that there exists a chain
>$$
>\mathfrak{p}_{0}\varsubsetneqq\mathfrak{p}_{1}\varsubsetneqq\cdots\varsubsetneqq\mathfrak{p}_{n}
>$$
>of prime ideals of $A$.
>
>(i) Prove that the Krull dimension of $\mathbb{C}[x]$ is 1.
>
>(ii) Prove that the Krull dimension of $\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]$ is at least $n$.
>
>(iii) Prove that $\mathbb{C}[x]$ is not isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}[x,y]$.

**(i)** Since $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed, any nonzero prime ideal is of the form $(x-a)$ for $a\in\mathbb{C}$. So Krull dimension is $1$.

**(ii)** Consider the chain of prime ideals:
$$
(0)\subsetneq(x_1)\subsetneq(x_1,x_2)\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq(x_1,\ldots,x_n)
$$
Each is prime because the quotient is a integral domain:
$$
\mathbb{C}[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(x_1,\ldots,x_k)\cong\mathbb{C}[x_{k+1},\ldots,x_n].
$$So Krull dimension is at least $n$.

**(iii)** $\mathbb{C}[x]$ has Krull dimension $1$, while $\mathbb{C}[x,y]$ has Krull dimension at least $2$, so they are not isomorphic.
