>[!problem] Problem 1
>Prove that the Laplacian
>$$
>\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}} + \cdots + \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}^{2}}
>$$
>on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is invariant under the action of $O(n)$.

**Proof:** Laplacian is a standard quadratic form, and $O(n)$ preserves standard quadratic form.

>[!problem] Problem 2
>Prove that linear differential operators on $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ that are invariant under the action of $O(2)$ are generated by the Laplacian.

**Proof:** Let $D$ be an $O(2)$-invariant linear differential operator on $\mathbb{R}^2$. In polar coordinates $(r,\theta)$, $O(2)$-invariance (rotations and reflections) implies $D$ contains only $r$-dependent coefficients and even powers of $\partial_\theta$. The only basic $O(2)$-invariant second-order operator is the Laplacian
$$\Delta = \partial_r^2 + \frac1r\partial_r + \frac1{r^2}\partial_\theta^2.$$
Since the algebra of invariant operators is isomorphic to the center of the universal enveloping algebra of the Euclidean group, which for $\mathbb{R}^2$ is generated by $\Delta$, any such $D$ is a polynomial in $\Delta$, i.e. $D=P(\Delta)$.

>[!problem] Problem 3
>Let $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$ be the 1-dimensional projective space
>$$
>\{[x : y] \mid x, y \in \mathbb{C},\, (x, y) 
eq (0, 0)\}/\sim
>$$
>where the equivalence relation is given by
>$$
>(x, y) \sim (\lambda x, \lambda y), \qquad \lambda \in \mathbb{C}^\times.
>$$
>Prove that the matrix multiplication
>$$
>\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}
>$$
>gives a left action of $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$ on $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$. Find the stabilizer of $[1 : 0]$.

**Proof of action:** Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in GL_2(\mathbb{C})$, $v = [x:y] \in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$.  
Define $A \cdot v = [ax+by : cx+dy]$. We must verify:

1. **Well-defineness**: If $[x:y] = [x':y']$, then $(x',y') = (\lambda x,\lambda y)$ for some $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}^\times$.  
   Then $A \cdot (x',y') = (\lambda(ax+by), \lambda(cx+dy)) = \lambda(ax+by, cx+dy) \sim (ax+by, cx+dy)$, so $A \cdot [x':y'] = A \cdot [x:y]$.

2. **Identity: $I_2 \cdot [x:y] = [x:y]$.

3. **Compatibility**: $(AB) \cdot [x:y] = [\text{(matrix product applied to }(x,y)^\top)] = A \cdot (B \cdot [x:y])$ by associativity of matrix multiplication.

Thus it defines a left action.

**Stabilizer of $[1:0]$**:  
$A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ fixes $[1:0]$ iff $[a : c] = [1:0]$, i.e. $(a,c) = (\lambda, 0)$ for some $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}^\times$.  
Then $A = \begin{pmatrix} \lambda & b \\ 0 & d \end{pmatrix}$ with $\det A = \lambda d = 0$, so $d = 0$.  
The stabilizer is
$$
\operatorname{Stab}([1:0]) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} \lambda & b \\ 0 & d \end{pmatrix} \mid \lambda, d \in \mathbb{C}^\times,\; b \in \mathbb{C} \right\}.
$$

>[!problem] Problem 4
>Try to find a set $S$ on which $GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})$ naturally acts and there is an element $s \in S$ whose stabilizer is the subgroup of $GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})$ consisting of upper triangular matrices.

**Set**: The set $S$ of complete flags in $\mathbb{C}^n$:
$$S = \{ \{0\}=V_0\subset V_1\subset\cdots\subset V_n=\mathbb{C}^n \mid \dim V_i=i \}.$$

**Action**: $GL_n(\mathbb{C})$ acts by $g\cdot (V_0\subset\cdots\subset V_n) = (gV_0\subset\cdots\subset gV_n)$.

**Element**: Take the standard flag $s = \big( \langle e_1\rangle \subset \langle e_1,e_2\rangle \subset\cdots\subset \langle e_1,\dots,e_n\rangle \big)$.

**Stabilizer**: 
$$
\operatorname{Stab}_{GL_n(\mathbb{C})}(s) = \{ g\in GL_n(\mathbb{C}) \mid gV_i = V_i \ \forall i \} = \text{upper triangular matrices}.
$$

>[!problem]
>Suppose there is a right action of a group $G$ on a set $S$.
>$$
>(s, g) \mapsto s \cdot g
>$$
>Let $C(S)$ be the set of $\mathbb{R}$-valued functions on $S$. We have two actions of $G$ on $C(S)$. For $f \in C(S)$, the formulae are given respectively by
>$$
>(g \cdot f)(s) = f(s \cdot g)
>$$
>$$
>(g \cdot f)(s) = f(s \cdot g^{-1})
>$$
>Determine which $G$-action is left and which $G$-action is right.

**Proof:**
First:
$$
(g_{1}g_{2})f(s)=f(s(g_{1}g_{2}))=f((sg_{1})g_{2})=g_{2}f(sg_{1})=g_{2}(g_{1}f)(s),
$$
thus right.

Second:
$$
(g_{1}g_{2})f(s)=f(s(g_{1}g_{2})^{-1})=f(sg_{2}^{-1}(g_{1}^{-1}))=g_{1}f(sg_{2}^{-1})=g_{1}(g_{2}f)(s),
$$
thus left.
