> [!problem] Problem 1
> Define the group
> $$SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \mid a, b, c, d \in \mathbb{Z},\ ad - bc = 1 \right\}.$$
> The group multiplication is matrix multiplication. Consider the two elements
> $$T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad S = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$
> Show that the group $SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})$ is generated by $T$ and $S$.

**Proof:**
Let $M = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ with $ad - bc = 1$.

**Step 1. Make $c = 0$.**
- If $|a| \ge |c| > 0$, we can add a multiple of the second row to the first row using powers of $T$ or $T^{-1}$.
  Multiply $M$ on the left by $T^{-k} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -k \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ for suitable integer $k$ to replace $(a,c)$ by $(a - kc, c)$. This is exactly the Euclidean algorithm on $(a,c)$. Repeating reduces $\max(|a|,|c|)$.
- When $|a| < |c|$, multiply on the left by $S = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, which swaps the rows (up to sign). This interchanges $|a|$ and $|c|$.
- By alternating these moves (row reduction + row swap), we eventually obtain a matrix with $c = 0$.

**Step 2. Matrix with $c = 0$.**
Now we have $\begin{pmatrix} a' & b' \\ 0 & d' \end{pmatrix}$ with $a'd' = 1$ (since determinant is 1 and $c=0$).
Hence $a' = d' = \pm 1$. Multiplying by $S^2 = -I$ if necessary, we may assume $a' = d' = 1$ (because $-I = S^2$ is in the subgroup generated by $S$).

Thus the matrix becomes $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & b' \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = T^{b'}$.

**Step 3. Conclusion.**
The whole process shows that applying a finite sequence of $T^{\pm 1}$ and $S$ to $M$ yields the identity. Hence $M$ itself is a word in $T$ and $S$.
Therefore $SL_2(\mathbb{Z}) = \langle T, S \rangle$.
