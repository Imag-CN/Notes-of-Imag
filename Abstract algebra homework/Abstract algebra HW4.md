> [!problem] Problem 1
> Define the group
> $$SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \mid a, b, c, d \in \mathbb{Z},\ ad - bc = 1 \right\}.$$
> The group multiplication is matrix multiplication. Consider the two elements
> $$T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad S = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$
> Show that the group $SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})$ is generated by $T$ and $S$.

**Proof:**
Let $M = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ with $ad-bc=1$.

While $c \neq 0$:  
- If $|a| \ge |c|$, left‑multiply by $T^{\mp k}$ to replace $(a,c)$ by $(a\mp kc,\,c)$ (choose sign to reduce $|a|$).  
- If $|a| < |c|$, left‑multiply by $S$ to swap the rows (up to sign).  
This is exactly the Euclidean algorithm on $(a,c)$, so after finitely many steps we obtain a matrix $\begin{pmatrix} a' & b' \\ c' & d' \end{pmatrix}$ with determinant $1$ and $a'=0$ or $c'=0$. We multiply the matrix by $S$ if $a'=0,c'\neq {0}$, otherwise do nothing.

From $a'd' = 1$ we have $a'=d'=\pm1$.  
If $a'=d'=-1$, left‑multiply by $S^2 = -I$ (which is in $\langle S,T \rangle$) to make $a'=d'=1$.  
Hence the matrix becomes $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & b' \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = T^{b'}$.

Since every step only used $T^{\pm1}$ and $S$, the original $M$ is a word in $T$ and $S$. Therefore $SL_2(\mathbb{Z}) = \langle T, S \rangle$.

> [!problem] Problem 2
> Show that the symmetric group $S_n$ is generated by $(12)$ and $(123\ldots n)$.

**Proof:**
Let $\sigma = (1\;2\;3\;\dots\;n)$ and $\tau = (1\;2)$.

We get all adjacent transpositions $(k\;k+1)$ from $\tau$ and $\sigma$ by conjugation:
$$
\sigma^k \tau \sigma^{-k} = (k+1,\;k+2) \qquad (k=0,1,\dots,n-2).
$$


Every permutation in $S_n$ can be written as a product of transpositions, and every transposition $(i\;j)$ ($i<j$) can be written as a product of adjacent transpositions:
$$
(i\;j) = (i\;i+1)(i+1\;i+2)\cdots(j-1\;j)\cdots(i+1\;i+2)(i\;i+1).
$$
Hence the adjacent transpositions generate $S_n$.

Therefore $S_n = \langle \tau,\;\sigma \rangle$.
